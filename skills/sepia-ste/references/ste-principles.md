# STE principles: technical and operational passages

These are editorial principles, not full ASD-STE100 compliance. They also work in Chinese; English word limits do not translate into Chinese character limits.

- Give prerequisites and relevant conditions before the action. Put warnings before hazardous steps; retain their risks and consequences.
- Use direct instructions, normally one action per sentence and one step per numbered item. Keep genuinely simultaneous actions together when separation would change the procedure.
- Prefer active voice with an explicit actor when known. Do not invent an actor to avoid passive voice.
- Use one term for one concept. Preserve approved project terminology, UI labels, commands, units, identifiers, and necessary repetition; do not replace them for variety.
- Keep sentences focused and pronouns unambiguous. Retain articles and other words needed for clarity. Avoid ambiguous noun clusters, participles, and implied conditions.
- For English, use approximately 20 words for instruction sentences and 25 for descriptive sentences as editing targets here, not automatic compliance tests. Split complex sentences instead of dropping conditions or evidence.
- Separate actions from explanations and expected results. Include failure handling and verification when relevant to the task and supported by evidence; never invent commands, outputs, or recovery steps.
- Natural contractions are optional in this non-strict mode only when appropriate to the venue. Consistency and clarity outrank Sepia's stylistic variety.

Before delivery, check that the reader knows what to do, under which conditions, and how to recognize the result. Do not add an STE label or compliance disclaimer to routine output unless relevant or requested.
